About Me

I'm Ayenew Shiferaw, a UX/UI designer with a multidisciplinary foundation in architecture, mobile app design, and human centered problem solving. I design with intention: every screen, flow, and interaction is shaped by clarity, structure, and empathy.

My journey began in architecture, where I learned how systems, space, and human behavior intersect. Today, I bring that same analytical mindset into digital design creating intuitive interfaces, scalable design systems, and thoughtful user experiences for real-world products. I've designed for fintech, travel, and education platforms, including mobile apps with complex user flows and transaction heavy interactions. Whether I'm conducting research, mapping journeys, wireframing, or prototyping in Figma, I focus on making products feel effortless and meaningful for the people who use them.

I thrive in collaborative, fast-moving environments and love transforming ideas into clean, functional, and visually engaging solutions. My goal is simple: design experiences that solve problems, reduce friction, and make life easier for users.

Hid-Go Flight Booking App

A redesigned mobile booking flow for domestic flights in Ethiopia — search, compare, and book in a few clear steps.

Overview

Hid-Go needed a mobile booking experience that could stand up to established travel apps. The existing flow asked too much of users too early, and the search-to-booking path had too many steps for what should be a quick task on a small screen.

Design process

A standard double-diamond approach, scaled for a solo practice project: understand the problem and the traveler, define what actually matters, explore options, design the screens, then test the flow against the personas below.

Problem statement

Travelers were dropping off midway through booking. Flight search results were dense and hard to scan, filters were buried, and the checkout flow mixed low-priority details in with the steps that actually mattered — picking a flight and paying. On mobile, this meant a lot of scrolling, backtracking, and abandoned sessions.

Who I designed for

Before sketching any screens, I defined four traveler personas based on common patterns in how people search for and book domestic flights. Each one pushed different priorities into the design.

The Frequent Flyer

Books often, wants speed and consistency.

Needs
Fast rebookingFlexible faresMileage tracking
Frustrations
Re-entering traveler info every tripSlow seat selection
Goals
Book in under 2 minutesKeep a saved traveler profile

The Budget Hunter

Price-driven, compares every option before paying.

Needs
Clear price comparisonFare breakdownNo hidden fees
Frustrations
Surprise fees at checkoutHard-to-compare fare tiers
Goals
Find the cheapest nonstop optionKnow exactly what's included

The Family Planner

Books for several people at once.

Needs

Frustrations

Goals

The First-Time Flyer

Unfamiliar with booking and airport terms.

Needs

Frustrations
Unfamiliar booking termsUncertainty about what happens next
Goals
Book without confusionKnow exactly what to do on travel day

Competitive analysis

A quick feature comparison against the two categories of apps travelers already use, to find where Hid-Go could genuinely be faster or clearer rather than just different.

FeatureHid-GoTypical airline appGeneric OTA app
Live seat map at booking✓✓—
Fare tiers shown before payment✓≈—
Fare + seats on one screen✓——
Saved traveler details✓✓≈
No desktop-only steps✓≈✓

Key insight

Across all four personas, the common thread was trust in the price shown. Whether someone was chasing the cheapest fare or just wanted to get through checkout without surprises, the fare tiers needed to be transparent and the total needed to stop changing after it was first shown. That single insight drove the fare and payment screens more than anything else.

Solution

The redesigned flow simplifies flight search into a cleaner, scannable results list, surfaces filters that travelers actually use, and streamlines checkout into a shorter, clearer sequence. Fare tiers and seat selection live on one screen so the price never shifts after that point, and the confirmation screen doubles as a boarding-pass-style record of the trip.

Guxo Bus Booking App

A sibling app to Hid-Go, extending the same booking patterns to intercity bus travel in Ethiopia.

Try the live prototype ↗

UI/UX Designer (solo)
Type
Self-directed practice project

Overview

Once Hid-Go's booking flow was working well, the natural next step was testing whether that same foundation could carry a different mode of travel. Guxo takes the flight-booking patterns from Hid-Go and rebuilds them for intercity bus travel, under its own visual identity and a fictional operator, "Guxo Line."

Design process

The same double-diamond approach as Hid-Go, run a second time to make sure the patterns held up for a genuinely different mode of travel rather than just a reskin.

Problem statement

A straight reskin wouldn't hold up. Bus travel has a different seat layout (2+2, not 3+3), a different route network built around major Ethiopian cities rather than airports, and different fare tiers that needed to make sense for a multi-hour road trip rather than a flight. It also needed to look and feel like its own product, not "Hid-Go in green." And unlike flights, most riders were booking through a walk-up counter or a phone call to an agent — not an app at all.

Who I designed for

Bus travelers in Ethiopia skew broader than flyers — more price-sensitive, more likely booking in person today. I defined four rider personas to keep the redesign honest about that gap.

The Daily Commuter

Rides the same route often, wants speed.

Needs
Quick repeat bookingReliable schedulesSeat availability at a glance
Frustrations
Long queues at the counterNo way to check seats before arriving
Goals
Book a seat in minutes from a phoneAvoid the counter entirely

The Budget Traveler

Price-sensitive, wary of inconsistent fares.

Needs
Lowest-fare visibilityClear fare tiersNo agent markups
Frustrations
Inconsistent pricing between agentsUnclear what Comfort vs. Standard includes
Goals
Pay a fair, transparent priceCompare fare tiers before choosing

The Family Traveler

Books several seats for a multi-hour trip.

Needs
Group seatingMulti-passenger checkoutClear luggage info
Frustrations
Being split across rowsBooking each family member separately
Goals
Sit together for the tripOne simple checkout for everyone

The First-Time Rider

Unfamiliar with routes and stations.

Needs
Clear route & station infoSimple seat pickerStraightforward confirmation
Frustrations
Not knowing which station to useUnfamiliar booking process
Goals
Book confidently without asking aroundKnow exactly where to go

Competitive analysis

Guxo isn't competing with other apps so much as with how bus tickets are actually bought today in Ethiopia.

FeatureGuxoWalk-up counterPhone / agent booking
Book without visiting a station✓—≈
See seat availability before paying✓——
Transparent, fixed pricing✓≈≈
Digital ticket / confirmation✓—≈
Choose your own seat✓——

Key insight

The personas and the competitive picture pointed to the same gap: riders don't currently get to see a seat before they own it. Whether it's a commuter checking availability or a family trying to sit together, being able to see and pick real seats — not just "a ticket" — was the one thing no existing option offered.

Solution

The result is a fully separate booking flow with its own brand: a fictional operator and an original bus glyph, rather than any real carrier's name or logo, plus an original illustrated hero scene in place of a stock photo. The seat map was rebuilt from 3+3 to a genuine 2+2 layout, fare tiers were renamed and re-priced for a road trip (Standard, Comfort, VIP), and every "flight" concept in the original code was traced through and replaced with its bus equivalent, down to the icon set and copy.
